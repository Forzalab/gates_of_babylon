# Variant research: romantic anime film craft (sprint 0930)

Goal: borrow real directors' shot grammar for 3 whole-flow variants. Each note ends in a rule we can use.

## Makoto Shinkai (Your Name, 5cm/s, Garden of Words): V1
- Themes are distance, time passing, missed connection. The sky and the weather tell the time for him.
  https://wou.omeka.net/s/repository/item/4650
- He uses lines in the frame (rails, doors, roofs) to cut two people apart, or to join them.
  https://filmdaze.substack.com/p/exploring-the-visual-themes-of-makoto
- The style is still and calm. Rain and light carry the feeling, and there is little talk.
  https://fictionmachine.com/2024/10/18/review-the-garden-of-words-2013-2/
- Rule: open each place on the sky or the light, and show time pass with a time-lapse sky. Let a hold (ma) do
  the emotion work, not a speech.

## Naoko Yamada (A Silent Voice, Liz and the Blue Bird): V2
- She tells the feeling through feet and hands, the "least expressive" body parts.
  https://filmotomy.com/femmefilmfest-review-a-silent-voice-naoko-yamada/
- Liz pushes realism until it breaks: small sounds up close, fidgets, a body that is shot like evidence.
  https://animetudes.com/2020/02/20/liz-and-the-blue-bird-beyond-realism/
- Happy feelings are body language too (fidgeting, a bounce in the step).
  https://letterboxd.com/greenmatty/film/liz-and-the-blue-bird/5/
- Rule: every choice pays off as a hand or feet insert. Faces come late. Sound is close and small (breath,
  fabric, a tap).

## Satoshi Kon (Perfect Blue, Paprika, Millennium Actress): V3
- "Editing Space & Time": match cuts join two places by a shared shape, so the scene changes and the eye does not.
  https://filmmakermagazine.com/86886-watch-satoshi-kon-editing-space-time/
- Perfect Blue uses match cuts to confuse on purpose. Paprika uses them to flow.
  https://otakufilms.wordpress.com/2015/02/18/satoshi-kon-techniques-and-utilizations-of-edits/
- Rule for us: use the Paprika kind (flow), not the Perfect Blue kind (confusion). A literal viewer must be told
  the new place by a stamp on the very next beat. One slip only, and she names it out loud.

## Mamoru Hosoda (The Girl Who Leapt Through Time)
- The same day replays, and each replay changes one small thing. Wide, static shots of the same spot make the
  repeat readable. https://film-cred.com/time-waits-for-no-one-mamoru-hosoda-the-girl-who-leapt-through-time/
- Rule for the loop: runs 2 and 3 reuse the SAME shot with ONE changed line, stated plainly.

## Mari Okada (anohana, Maquia)
- She builds tears from small objects that come back (a promise, a cloth). Theme, story, and picture share one
  object. https://mageinabarrel.com/2018/11/12/tragedy-and-healing-why-maquia-makes-us-cry/
- Rule: every object Nanda touches early (bento, key, cups) must be seen again at the house.

## Clarity (screenwriting craft)
- Setups and payoffs: plant the object plainly, then pay it off where the stakes are higher.
  https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/how-to-put-setups-and-payoffs-in-your-scripts
- Enter the scene late, leave early, keep dialogue short. https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/planning/writing/the-power-of-words-tips-for-writing-strong-dialogue/
- Action lines stay compact: one image per line. https://www.kinolime.com/learn/strong-action-lines
- Rule: one idea per line, a literal subject + verb, and no line explains a joke.

## Extra shot grammar (beyond the rubric menu)
- Graphic match (shape to shape: bento lid, sun, clock face). Kon.
- Feet insert (walking pace = mood). Yamada.
- Empty-frame hold: the place stays on screen after the people leave. Shinkai / Ozu "pillow shot".
- Sound bridge: the next place's sound starts on the old picture (J-cut). All three.
- Repeat framing: the same shot on runs 1, 2, 3, one change each time. Hosoda.
- Shot ids here come from `SHOTS-SCHEMA.md` on sprint/seq-visual (stamp, establish, insert, closeup, reaction,
  push, rack, pov, ots, timelapse, match, dutch).
